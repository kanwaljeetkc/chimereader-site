The case for reading slowly

Reading · September 2026

Speed reading sells transport; comprehension is not a destination

Speed reading sells because it treats reading as transport: get from the first page to the last in less time. For a timetable that is exactly right. For anything you intend to keep, it mistakes the destination for the point.

Difficult prose is difficult because the ideas are entangled, and untangling them takes the time it takes. A reader who covers ten pages and retains one argument has done better than a reader who covers fifty and retains a mood.

The useful discipline is not slowness for its own sake but honesty about comprehension. Stop at the end of a section and say what it claimed. If you cannot, going faster will not help.

This is also the strongest argument for hearing a text rather than scanning it. A voice cannot skim. It moves at the pace of sentences, which is roughly the pace at which sentences were meant to be understood.
