An argument for finishing

Reading · September 2026

Starting is cheap. The information is all in the last ten per cent

Starting is pleasant and cheap. Every new project arrives with its difficulties still hidden, and the first hour is spent entirely on the parts you already know how to do.

Finishing is where the information is. The last ten per cent contains every problem your plan failed to anticipate, and it is the only part that teaches you something you could not have guessed. A person who has finished five small things knows more than one who has begun fifty.

So the advice is narrow but firm: make the thing smaller until you can finish it, then finish it. Scope is adjustable. Completion is the part that cannot be faked.

It follows that the most valuable habit is not ambition but amputation — the willingness to cut a project down to something that can actually cross the line this week.
