On walking and thinking

Reading · September 2026

Why the best ideas arrive somewhere between the kerb and the corner

There is a reason so many people report their best thinking on a walk, and it is not mystical. Walking occupies exactly enough attention to stop you circling one thought, and not so much that you cannot hold an idea at all.

A desk offers the opposite arrangement. Nothing competes for attention, so a single anxious thought can run unopposed for an hour. The walk supplies mild, continuous interruption — a kerb, a corner, a change of light — and each one loosens the grip of whatever you were stuck on.

This is also why the walk fails if you spend it composing. Deliberate work restores the grip you went out to break. The trick is to hold the question loosely and let the street do the rest.

The practical form of this is narrow. Leave without a notebook, or at least without opening it. Carry one question, not three. And accept that most walks return nothing, because the ones that do are paying for the ones that did not.
